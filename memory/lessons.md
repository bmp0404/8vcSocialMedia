# Lessons

**Evidence base:** 10 reviewed drafts (7 scored 1.0, 2 scored 0.7). There are no published-post metrics, no human notes, and no reason codes. Everything below comes from review scores and critic comments, so treat it as provisional. Real engagement data should override it as soon as it exists.

## What worked (reviewed 1.0)

1. **Lead with the concrete collision.** "Two agents in auth.ts / schema.sql" appeared in the top posts and replies. Use a specific file, a specific number, and a specific moment, not abstractions like "coordination challenges."
2. **Short thesis posts that end on a one-line punchline.** "Throughput was never the bottleneck. Coordination was." Cut setup so the last line arrives sooner.
3. **"You are the handoff."** Naming the human as the missing infrastructure was a strong hook. Reuse the pattern of a 3-beat pain followed by one reframing line.
4. **Memes and in-character bits land when they are relatable.** The "nobody: / my 5 agents at 2am" format and the "agent 14, checking in" reply both scored 1.0. Specific numbers that feel like a confession (7 agents) beat round ones.
5. **Replies that add an opinion or a joke before any product mention.** The best replies had no product claim, or only a light one. Put the joke or the point first and end on the best line.
6. **Vendor-neutral framing works.** Keep it about the coordination layer being separate from any one agent. It is a real opinion, but name the concrete pain instead of being cryptic ("so a deal doesn't break your setup" was flagged).
7. **Demo and install posts should open with the objection-killer.** Lead with "works locally, no account, never uploads a transcript, never pushes code," then the install command.

## What to tighten or stop

8. **Don't hard-sell in someone else's replies.** The reply that listed six tools plus "Coordigent does that" was flagged. Trim tool lists to a few names, or drop them, and lead with the joke.
9. **Make the product line one short beat.** In the pain stories, "Coordigent does that" should not read as an ad. One sentence, after the pain has landed.
10. **Don't bury the punchline under framing.** The timeline post (0.7) had a strong last line ("a whole afternoon of reviewing it") but diluted it with the "4x the code" setup. Make the strongest line the ending.
11. **First-person agent jokes scored lower (0.7) than the plain-spoken thesis (1.0) in posts.** The bit is funny but less sharp, so keep it to replies and memes, where it scored 1.0, or make the joke land faster.
12. **Keep to supported claims only.** Every approved product claim matched the fact sheet. Don't invent features, numbers, or integrations.
